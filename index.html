LiDAR performance in rainfall

Start with the rain-car cover, then swipe through six short engineering takeaways.

ASSURANCE BRIEFS · SENSORS1 / 7
ENGINEERING INSIGHTS

When rain meets LiDAR.

What a controlled rainfall study tells us about sensor validation.

01 · THE PROBLEM2 / 7

What does rain change for LiDAR?

A sensor that performs well in clear weather may return weaker signals in rain. We need evidence across conditions—not just a headline range specification.

02 · TEST SETUP3 / 7

Vary the conditions, not just the target.

The researchers tested a VLP-32 in a darkroom at 10–30 m, rainfall from 0–40 mm/h, and viewing angles of 0°, 30°, and 60°.

03 · THE METRICS4 / 7

Intensity and point count tell different stories.

The study tracked reflected-signal intensity and point-cloud counts. Both help characterize what the sensor actually records.

04 · KEY RESULTS5 / 7

Reflectivity and distance are critical.

A 5%-reflective reference target produced no recorded intensity or points beyond 25 m. High-reflectivity targets also showed large intensity losses under combined harsh conditions.

05 · MY TAKEAWAY6 / 7

A single passing test is not an assurance case.

I would map verification across distance, surface reflectivity, incidence angle, and rainfall—then document the limits and uncertainty of the evidence.

06 · LIMITATIONS7 / 7

A controlled test is not the whole road.

The findings concern one LiDAR model in simulated rainfall. Natural weather, longer range, and other sensor designs still require separate validation.

How do we know if an autonomous vehicle made the right decision?

Read original source ↗
ASSURANCE BRIEFS · PHYSICAL AI1 / 7
🚗
NIST · TECHNICAL PROGRAM

Did the AV make the right call?

Measuring the quality of automated-driving decisions.

01 · THE GAP2 / 7

Passing a scenario doesn't prove good judgment.

An automated vehicle can avoid a collision but still choose a poorer maneuver. NIST is asking how to measure the quality of that decision.

02 · THE SETUP3 / 7

Test the decisions without reading the code.

NIST describes a plug-in test harness linking simulated scenarios, a physical research vehicle, and automated driving systems evaluated as black boxes.

03 · FOUR TIERS4 / 7

Move from outcomes to decision quality.

The plan starts with safety metrics, adds predicted outcomes, compares actions with counterfactual baselines, and looks for recurring weaknesses.

04 · THE SCENARIOS5 / 7

Realistic risk needs representative situations.

The proposed test scenarios draw from real crash data. Simulation and physical-vehicle checks are intended to help establish meaningful evidence.

05 · MY TAKEAWAY6 / 7

Assurance should ask: was there a safer choice?

I would connect scenario requirements to decisions, available alternatives, acceptance thresholds and evidence from the entire operating envelope.

06 · THE LIMIT7 / 7

This is a research program, not a proven certification method.

NIST describes planned methods and tools. Performance thresholds and regulatory acceptance still need to be established.

ENGINEERING INSIGHTS

Can AI monitoring actually guarantee safety?

AI observability needs more than an uptime dashboard.

01 · THE PROBLEM2 / 7

A healthy dashboard can hide an unsafe decision.

A model may be accurate on average and still lack the information needed to choose a safe action.

02 · THE FRAMEWORK3 / 7

Look at what the system can actually observe.

The authors model hidden states, observed signals and the actions that remain feasible under uncertainty.

03 · THREE REGIMES4 / 7

Not every system is equally deployable.

The paper separates directly deployable systems, systems needing safeguards, and systems that cannot be safely deployed as designed.

04 · MONITORING LIMITS5 / 7

You cannot monitor what you never measure.

If safety-critical information remains hidden, monitoring based on the same signals cannot establish a safety guarantee.

05 · MY TAKEAWAY6 / 7

Treat observability as an assurance requirement.

In my view, verification should connect observable signals, safety decisions, triggers, and fallback actions.

06 · THE LIMIT7 / 7

A framework is not a validated product.

This is primarily a formal deployment framework, not a field trial of automotive sensing. Apply it with domain-specific evidence.

ENGINEERING INSIGHTS

Measurement uncertainty is part of sensor evidence

A test value without uncertainty can be misleading.

01 · THE PROBLEM2 / 7

More sensors do not automatically mean more certainty.

Networks combine measurements made at different places, times and conditions. Their outputs still carry uncertainty.

02 · THE RESEARCH3 / 7

Treat the network as a measurement system.

The paper surveys approaches to evaluating uncertainty for distributed sensor-network measurements.

03 · THE CHALLENGE4 / 7

Uncertainty can enter at several layers.

Individual sensors, their calibration and the way their measurements are combined all matter to the final result.

04 · THE IMPLICATION5 / 7

A fused number needs defensible confidence.

If a network informs a safety or compliance decision, the resulting uncertainty needs to be understood and reported.

05 · MY TAKEAWAY6 / 7

Build uncertainty into V&V evidence.

I would link each reported metric to calibration, reference equipment, environment and processing assumptions.

06 · THE LIMIT7 / 7

No single uncertainty budget fits every system.

The paper discusses sensor-network metrology broadly; each automotive sensor architecture needs a tailored evaluation.

ENGINEERING INSIGHTS

Testing perception weaknesses before they become hazards

A practical way to think about SOTIF verification.

01 · THE PROBLEM2 / 7

A system can be fault-free and still behave unsafely.

For automated driving, limitations of intended perception behavior can create risk even when hardware is functioning.

02 · THE APPROACH3 / 7

Inject perception insufficiencies deliberately.

The authors explore SOTIF validation by introducing perception performance insufficiencies into automated-driving evaluations.

03 · WHAT IT TESTS4 / 7

Move beyond perfect perception assumptions.

Controlled degradation helps reveal how downstream driving behavior responds when environmental understanding is incomplete.

04 · WHY IT MATTERS5 / 7

A perception error is a system-level question.

The useful evidence is not merely that a sensor misses something, but how the automated system responds.

05 · MY TAKEAWAY6 / 7

Trace sensor limits to driving outcomes.

I would map an insufficiency to the scenario, observable response, acceptance criteria and residual safety concern.

06 · THE LIMIT7 / 7

Scenario coverage remains the challenge.

Injecting insufficiencies helps probe behavior, but it cannot prove that every real-world hazardous scenario is covered.

ENGINEERING INSIGHTS

Why autonomous-system verification needs multiple methods

A research review of testing and verification for connected and autonomous vehicles.

01 · THE PROBLEM2 / 7

No single test campaign can cover every driving situation.

Connected and autonomous vehicles combine software, sensors, communications and decisions that interact in complex ways.

02 · THE REVIEW3 / 7

Different verification methods answer different questions.

This review examines testing and verification approaches for connected and autonomous vehicle systems.

03 · THE CHALLENGE4 / 7

Simulation and physical testing both have limits.

Representative scenarios, assumptions and the gap between models and real environments influence what each result proves.

04 · THE EVIDENCE5 / 7

Validation needs traceability across layers.

Component results help, but the system case also depends on integration, realistic scenarios and defined operating limits.

05 · MY TAKEAWAY6 / 7

Use risk to choose the verification mix.

I would combine laboratory, simulation, integration and vehicle evidence based on hazards and failure modes.

06 · THE LIMIT7 / 7

A survey identifies approaches, not universal coverage.

The paper summarizes methods; it does not establish that any one test strategy is sufficient for every platform.
